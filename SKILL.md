# Z Creative Asset Critique

Use this skill to give practical, low-friction feedback on a supplied creative asset. Help the requester move forward. Preserve effective work and recommend the smallest change that fixes a demonstrated problem.

## Native tool use
For a Canva design, use the available Canva review skill and connector to inspect it; apply this skill's business verdict without repeating a second full review. For uploaded/local images use the host's image viewer. Label supplied descriptions as reported evidence rather than claiming direct visual inspection.
Creation or editing is a separate authorized action routed to the available ImageGen, Canva or creative-production skill.

## Use This Skill

Use this skill when a requester asks whether a marketing graphic, photo, logo, ad, hero image, offer sheet, or text-heavy visual works, needs revisions, or is ready to publish.

Do not use this skill to create or edit creative assets, publish them, access Canva credentials, certify legal rights, run a complete accessibility audit, or approve dimensions, links, QR codes, print settings, or exports that were not actually checked.

## Start With a Quick Look

Begin with a **Quick Look** unless the requester explicitly asks for a deeper review or the asset is clearly a final, costly, or important publication.

Do not require a form or full brief before giving visible feedback. State an assumption and ask one concise question only when the answer could change the result. Give a provisional review if the answer is unavailable.

When the requester supplies an asset or a list of observed facts, treat that information as the review evidence. Do not turn missing information into a defect. Do not invent a missing logo, CTA, disclaimer, contrast problem, brand problem, or platform requirement. If an unverified item matters, name it as a final check rather than a reason to fail the visible asset.

Use these levels internally. Do not require a VA to choose or understand them.

| Level | Use when | Output size |
|---|---|---|
| Quick Look | Default for ordinary graphics, photographs, ads, hero images, social posts, and assets with text. | Short paragraph and up to four bullets. |
| Focus Review | The requester asks for a deeper look, or a specific issue needs closer inspection. | Purpose-specific fixes and handoff notes. |
| Pre-Flight Review | Perform final readiness review for costly or important publishing, including major ads, large print, new logo systems, or key landing-page heroes. | Must-fix items, production notes, and ship checks. |

## Review the Asset

1. Identify the asset type and its visible purpose. Treat the stated use as authoritative. If the use is unknown, say what you assumed.
2. Inspect what is actually visible. Separate a visible observation from an unverified claim. Mention uncertainty only when it affects the advice.
3. Preserve what already works. Do not redesign for personal taste.
4. Use the relevant checklist in [asset checklists](references/asset-checklists.md). Read [channel and production notes](references/channel-and-production-notes.md) only when the stated channel or supplier matters.
5. Choose the lightest review level that can answer the request. Do not deepen the review merely because an ad has text or appears on a known channel.
6. Prioritize material problems. Report every separate issue that can impair understanding, accuracy, trust, or intended use. Do not pad the response with minor preferences.

## Write the Review

For every Quick Look, use at most 100 words and provide only the relevant elements in plain language:

- **Verdict:** Use **Ready**, **Fix**, or **Rebuild** and give one brief reason.
- **Keep:** Name the part that is doing its job.
- **Change:** Give the most important executable fix, if one is needed. Include every additional material issue.
- **Done when:** State the observable condition that ends the revision.

When no material visible problem exists, use **Ready** or **Visually ready**. Do not add a change merely to appear helpful. If an untested technical step remains, label it **Final check**, not **Change**. Do not end a pass-case review with a follow-up question when one generic final check can cover the uncertainty. Ask a follow-up question only when the answer is necessary to choose the verdict.

Use **Visually ready** when the composition, copy hierarchy, and visible content pass, but an untested technical or production check remains. Do not imply that an export, link, QR code, dimensions, or print setup passed unless it was checked.

For a Focus Review or Pre-Flight Review, add only the relevant items: replacement copy, ordered fixes, source-confirmation request, channel notes, export guidance, launch measurement, or unresolved questions. Use [response patterns](references/response-patterns.md) for concise formats.

Use confidence wording only when uncertainty could change the decision. Keep it to one short sentence and do not repeat the verdict reason.

## Apply Asset-Specific Checks

Read only the appropriate section of [asset checklists](references/asset-checklists.md).

- Check logos and wordmarks for recognition, small-size readability, and usable relationships among icon, name, and tagline.
- Check photographs for subject, focus, lighting, colour, crop, distracting backgrounds, usable text space, audience fit, and visible editing or AI-image defects. Judge the photo for its actual job. A useful jobsite photo does not need studio polish.
- Check hero images and website graphics for headline-image fit, reading order, mobile legibility, and the clarity of the next customer step after relevance is established.
- Check ads for fast message clarity, offer support, brand recognition, readable text, and channel-safe areas when verified channel requirements are available.
- Check offer sheets and text-heavy graphics for clear reading order, copy clarity, contrast, mobile collapse, and whether the visual supports the message rather than buries it.

## Review Revisions

When reviewing a revised asset, check the previous fixes first. Close issues that are resolved. Raise a new issue only if the revision introduced it or it materially affects intended use.

Do not reopen settled design preferences or create another round only to suggest more polish.

## Set Boundaries and Stop Conditions

State when a source limitation prevents certainty. For example, ask for the source file to confirm a font, a mobile screenshot to judge small-size legibility, or a supplier specification to approve print production.

Do not invent exact type sizes, font names, colour values, clear-space rules, platform specifications, legal rights, or competitor similarity. Do not claim direct Canva access unless it is available in the current session.

Do not make a production change or publish an asset. If the requester needs asset editing, generation, publishing, legal review, or full accessibility testing, identify the next responsible role or skill.

## Verify Completion

Before ending a review, confirm that the verdict matches the visible evidence, material findings have an executable fix, working elements are preserved, and the response says what must happen before the asset is fully ready.

A review succeeds when it leaves good work alone, catches meaningful problems, gives the VA or designer a clear next action, and makes the stopping point obvious.
