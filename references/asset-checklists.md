# Asset Checklists

Use only the section that matches the asset. These are review prompts, not a mandatory audit.

## Logo, Wordmark, or Lockup

Review whether the mark is recognizable, whether the business name reads at the smallest relevant size, and whether the icon, wordmark, and tagline have a clear relationship. Flag a tagline only if it harms recognition or becomes unreadable. Request a small-size sample when the intended placement is unknown.

Do not invent official clear-space, font, colour, or minimum-size rules from a flattened asset. Ask for the approved brand guide or source file when confirmation matters.

## Photograph

Review the subject, focus, exposure or lighting, colour, crop, distracting backgrounds, and available space for overlaid text. Check whether the image fits the stated audience, service, and channel. Look for visible editing errors or AI-image problems such as implausible hands, warped tools, broken lettering, impossible shadows, or inconsistent reflections.

Judge the image against its purpose. A credible jobsite, agricultural, or local-business photo may be more useful than polished studio imagery. Recommend a replacement only when the photograph cannot serve the intended job through a bounded crop or retouch.

## Hero Image or Website Graphic

Check whether the image and headline reinforce the same message, whether the headline remains the first meaningful element, and whether the layout leaves a sensible next step after relevance is established. Review mobile legibility only from a supplied mobile rendering or a clearly comparable source. Otherwise, state that final mobile rendering needs checking.

## Ad Creative

Check whether a viewer can quickly understand who the message is for, what is offered, and what action makes sense after reading the offer. Check readable copy, offer support, brand recognition, and any stated channel safe area. Do not add urgency, scarcity, testimonials, or badges that are absent from the actual offer solely because they are common marketing tactics.

## One-Page Offer Sheet

Check whether a reader can follow the content from need or problem to proof, offer, and next action when those elements are relevant. Judge the reading order against the asset's actual purpose. Do not force a universal sales framework onto an informational or identity-focused document.

## Text-Heavy Graphic

Check headline clarity, reading order, contrast, line breaks, scanability, and whether decorative elements compete with the message. Flag the first element that will fail at the smallest real viewing size. Give an executable fix such as shortening the headline, increasing contrast, moving a background image, or separating two competing messages.

## Multi-Asset Pack

Review consistency only across the supplied assets. Check the repeated brand elements, copy hierarchy, crop treatment, and whether each file has a distinguishable job. Do not claim competitor sameness or system-wide inconsistency without the relevant comparison set.
